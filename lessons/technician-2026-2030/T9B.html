The feed line carries your signal from the radio to the antenna. Choosing the right cable and connectors, and keeping them dry, makes a real difference to your signal.

Coaxial cable

"Coax" is the round cable with a center wire inside a shield. It's the most common feed line because it's easy to use and needs few special installation considerations. Most ham coax is 50 ohms.

  • Coax loss increases as frequency goes up, which is why cable choice matters most on UHF.
  • RG-213 (thick) has less loss than RG-58 (thin) at the same frequency.
  • Air-insulated hardline has the lowest loss of all, but it's stiff and expensive.
  • Loss comes from all three: water getting into connectors, high SWR, and lots of connectors in the line.

SWR again

SWR is a measure of how well a load (the antenna) is matched to the transmission line. If SWR jumps around erratically, look for a loose connection in the antenna or feed line.

An antenna tuner (antenna coupler) matches the antenna system's impedance to the transceiver's output impedance, so the radio sees a good match.

Outdoors, tape every kind carefully for weather protection.

Key facts

  • Coax is common because it's easy to use. Typical impedance: 50 ohms.
  • Loss rises with frequency. RG-213 has less loss than RG-58. Air-insulated hardline is lowest.
  • Loss sources: water intrusion, high SWR, many connectors.
  • SWR = how well the load matches the line. Erratic SWR = a loose connection.
  • Tuner matches the antenna system to the transceiver.
  • PL-259: HF/VHF. Type N: above 400 MHz. Tape all outdoor connectors.

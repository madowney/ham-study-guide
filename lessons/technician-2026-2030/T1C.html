This lesson covers the paperwork side of being a ham: license classes, call signs, how long a license lasts, renewal, and when you can first transmit.

License classes

The FCC issues new licenses in exactly three classes: Technician, General, and Amateur Extra. Older classes (Novice, Technician Plus, Advanced) still exist for people who hold them, but nobody can get a new one.

Call signs

A U.S. call sign is a prefix (starting with A, K, N or W), one digit, and a suffix. New Technicians are issued a "Group D" call sign: two letters, a digit, three letters, like KF1XXX. Shorter calls such as W1XX or KA1X belong to higher classes.

Under the vanity call sign program, any licensed amateur can request a specific call sign, within the formats their license class is eligible for.

How long a license lasts

  • Normal term: ten years.
  • You can apply to renew up to 90 days before it expires.
  • If it expires, there's a two-year grace period to renew without retesting. But during the grace period you may not transmit until the license has actually been renewed.

Your first transmission

Passing the exam is not enough to get on the air. You may transmit as soon as your license grant appears in the FCC's license database (ULS). Not when you get your exam certificate (the CSCE), and not when the ARRL's website shows it.

The FCC contacts you by email, so keep your email address current. If the FCC can't reach you by email, it can revoke your station license or suspend your operator license.

Talking to the world

You may talk with hams in other countries. International contacts must be about things incidental to the purposes of amateur radio, plus remarks of a personal character: your station, the weather, your family. Business talk is out.

You can operate from a ship in international waters if it's a U.S.-documented vessel and you have the master's (captain's) permission.

Key facts

  • New licenses: Technician, General, Amateur Extra only.
  • Technician call sign format: 2×3, e.g. KF1XXX. Any licensee may request a vanity call.
  • Term 10 years; renew up to 90 days early; 2-year grace period with no transmitting.
  • Transmit once your grant is in the FCC database.
  • No email contact can lead to revocation or suspension.
  • International contacts: ham-radio topics and personal remarks only.
  • At sea: U.S.-documented vessel, with the master's permission.

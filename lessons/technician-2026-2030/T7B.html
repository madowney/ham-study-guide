Sooner or later you'll hear "you're breaking up" or "you're on my TV." This lesson covers the usual causes of bad audio and interference, and the polite, practical fixes.

When your signal sounds bad

  • FM over-deviating (too much swing): talk farther from the microphone.
  • Distorted or unintelligible through a repeater: it could be any of these: you're slightly off frequency, you're too loud or too close to the mic, or you're in a bad location.
  • Distortion from RF getting back into the radio: clip a ferrite choke on the microphone cable to keep the transmitted signal from feeding back in.
  • Low power output from a modern solid-state radio is often caused by high SWR. You'll learn about SWR in the next lesson.

Kinds of interference

Radio frequency interference (RFI) can come from all three of these:

  • Fundamental overload: your strong signal swamps a nearby receiver that can't reject it.
  • Harmonics: unwanted copies of your signal at multiples of your frequency.
  • Spurious emissions: any other unwanted signals your transmitter produces.

A neighbor's AM or FM radio picks up your transmission because the receiver can't reject strong signals outside its band. That's a receiver problem, not a transmitter problem.

Fixing it

  • Your signal in their receiver: put a filter at the antenna input of the affected receiver to block your signal.
  • A neighbor complains: first make sure your station is working properly, and that it doesn't interfere with your own radio or TV on the same channel.
  • Cable TV interference: first make sure all the TV coax connectors are installed properly.
  • A strong commercial FM station bothering your 2-meter radio: install a band-reject filter.
  • Something in the neighbor's house interfering with you: all three steps apply: work with them to find the device, politely explain that the FCC prohibits devices that cause interference, and make sure your own station meets good amateur practice.

Key facts

  • Over-deviating: talk farther from the mic. Feedback distortion: a ferrite choke on the mic cable.
  • Low output from a solid-state radio: high SWR.
  • RFI causes: fundamental overload, harmonics, spurious emissions.
  • A broadcast radio hearing you: it can't reject strong out-of-band signals. Filter at its antenna input.
  • Neighbor complaint: first check that your station is OK and doesn't affect your own TV.
  • Cable TV: check the connectors first. Commercial FM into 2 m: band-reject filter.

Your license gives you specific slices of the radio spectrum. As a Technician you get everything above 50 MHz, plus small pieces of four HF bands. This lesson is the map.

Technician privileges at a glance

The parts the exam asks about

  • Voice on HF: Technicians have phone privileges on the 10-meter band only, from 28.300 to 28.500 MHz.
  • Digital modes like FT8: allowed on 10 meters, 6 meters and 2 meters (and the other VHF/UHF bands).
  • Which band is it? 52.525 MHz is in 6 meters (50–54). 146.52 MHz is in 2 meters (144–148); it's the national 2-meter calling frequency. 49 MHz and 222.15 MHz are not 6 meters.
  • CW-only segments above 50 MHz: the bottom 100 kHz of 6 and 2 meters, 50.0–50.1 MHz and 144.0–144.1 MHz.
  • SSB voice above 50 MHz is allowed in at least some segment of every band.
  • The ISS: any Technician or higher may contact the International Space Station on VHF. No NASA approval is needed.

Power limits

Power is measured as PEP (peak envelope power), the strongest point of your signal.

  • Technician on the HF slices: 200 watts PEP.
  • Above 30 MHz: up to 1500 watts PEP, with a few specific exceptions. In practice most handheld radios put out 5 watts.

Sharing the spectrum

On some bands, amateurs are secondary users. Another service, often government radar, has priority. There you may hear non-amateur stations, and you must not interfere with them.

Don't park your transmitter exactly on a band edge. All three reasons the exam gives are right: your radio's display might be slightly off (calibration error), the frequency can drift, and your signal is wider than a single frequency. Its sidebands spill a little to each side, so part of it could land outside the band.

Key facts

  • Tech HF voice: 10 m only, 28.300–28.500 MHz. HF power limit 200 W PEP.
  • Above 30 MHz: 1500 W PEP. CW-only: 50.0–50.1 and 144.0–144.1 MHz.
  • 6 m = 50–54 MHz (52.525 MHz). 2 m = 144–148 MHz (146.52 MHz).
  • FT8 and other digital modes: 10, 6 and 2 meters all OK.
  • SSB is allowed somewhere in every band above 50 MHz.
  • Any Technician may contact the ISS.
  • Secondary allocation: avoid interfering with the primary users.
  • Stay off band edges: calibration error, drift and sidebands.

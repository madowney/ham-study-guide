Every station has to say who it is, regularly and in a set way. This lesson covers station identification, plus third-party traffic, repeaters, club licenses and inspections.

Identifying your station

The rule is easy to remember: give your call sign at least every 10 minutes during a contact, and at the end of it. You don't have to say it at the start, though most people do.

  • By voice (phone), identify in English.
  • A phone station may identify by voice or in Morse code (CW).
  • Tactical call signs, like "Race Headquarters" at a marathon, are fine for coordination, but you still give your FCC call sign every 10 minutes and at the end.
  • If you're operating away from home, you can add an indicator after your call. By voice, "stroke," "slant" or "slash" are all acceptable: "KL7CC slash W3."

Third-party communications

A third-party communication is a message from one control operator to another, on behalf of someone else. Letting a non-ham friend talk on your radio is one, and so is passing a message for someone.

A non-licensed person may talk to a ham in another country through your station only if that country has a third-party agreement with the U.S. You stay the control operator throughout.

Repeaters

A repeater receives a signal on one channel and simultaneously retransmits it on another, usually from a high spot so handhelds can reach far. If something illegal gets retransmitted, it's the control operator of the station that originated it who's accountable, not the repeater's owner.

Clubs and inspections

  • A club station license requires a club with at least four members.
  • The station licensee must make the station and its records available at any time when an FCC representative asks. No warrant or advance notice is needed.

Key facts

  • Identify at least every 10 minutes and at the end, in English for phone, by voice or CW.
  • Tactical calls don't replace your FCC call sign.
  • "Stroke," "slant" and "slash" are all acceptable.
  • Third party = a message on behalf of another person; it needs a third-party agreement when it's international.
  • Repeater = simultaneous retransmission on another channel. The originating operator is accountable.
  • Club license: at least 4 members. FCC inspection: any time on request.

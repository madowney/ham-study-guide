How far will your signal go? On VHF and UHF, usually to the horizon. On HF, potentially around the world. This lesson covers the main ways signals travel, including the occasional surprises.

Line of sight and the radio horizon

VHF and UHF signals mostly travel in straight lines. UHF simplex signals rarely go beyond the radio horizon, because UHF usually isn't bent back by the ionosphere. The radio horizon is a bit farther than the visual horizon, because the atmosphere bends (refracts) radio waves slightly around the curve of the Earth.

Knife-edge diffraction lets signals bend over the sharp edge of an obstruction, like a ridge or building, to reach a station behind it.

HF: around the world

The big difference between HF and VHF/UHF: long-distance ionospheric propagation is far more common on HF.

  • The ionosphere's highest layer, the F region, does the long-distance work.
  • It's strongest when there are lots of sunspots. Sunspots come and go in an 11-year cycle.
  • Best time for long-distance 10 meters: from dawn to shortly after sunset, during high sunspot activity. The sun powers the ionosphere, so daytime is when it works.
  • At the peak of the sunspot cycle, even 6 and 10 meters can go worldwide.

VHF/UHF surprises

PropagationWhat happens
Sporadic EPatches of the ionosphere's E region suddenly reflect signals, giving occasional strong signals beyond the horizon on 10, 6 and 2 meters
Tropospheric ductingCaused by temperature inversions (warm air above cool air), which trap signals in a "duct." Gives regular VHF/UHF contacts out to about 300 miles.
Meteor scatterSignals bounce off the brief ionized trails of meteors. Best band: 6 meters.
Auroral backscatterSignals bounce off the northern lights. They sound distorted, with a raspy sound.

Key facts

  • UHF stays near the horizon because the ionosphere doesn't usually return it.
  • The radio horizon is beyond the visual horizon because the atmosphere refracts radio waves.
  • Knife-edge diffraction gets signals past obstructions.
  • HF = far more long-distance ionospheric propagation.
  • 10 m by the F region: dawn to just after sunset, with high sunspots. At the sunspot peak: 6 and 10 m.
  • Sporadic E: occasional strong signals on 10, 6 and 2 m.
  • Tropo ducting: temperature inversions, about 300 miles.
  • Meteor scatter: 6 m. Aurora: raspy signals.

What's inside a radio? This lesson names the building blocks of receivers and transmitters, and the add-ons people connect to them.

Receiver qualities

  • Sensitivity: the ability to detect a weak signal at all.
  • Selectivity: the ability to separate one signal from others nearby.

Sensitivity notices; selectivity selects.

Building blocks

CircuitWhat it does
OscillatorGenerates a signal at a specific frequency
VFO (variable frequency oscillator)An adjustable oscillator that sets the receive and transmit frequency. It's the tuning knob's circuit.
MixerConverts a signal from one frequency to another
ModulatorCombines speech with the RF carrier (that's modulation)

The radio and its add-ons

  • A transceiver combines a receiver and a transmitter in one box.
  • Its PTT (push-to-talk) input switches it from receive to transmit when grounded, for example when you press the microphone button.
  • A transverter converts a transceiver's RF input and output to a different band, say from 10 meters up to a microwave band.
  • An RF power amplifier goes on the transceiver's output to increase transmitted power.
  • Some VHF amplifiers have an SSB / CW-FM switch. It sets the amplifier for proper operation in the selected mode; it doesn't change your radio's mode.

Key facts

  • Sensitivity = detects a signal. Selectivity = separates signals.
  • Oscillator generates a frequency. VFO sets the receive and transmit frequency. Mixer changes frequency.
  • Modulation = combining speech with the RF carrier.
  • Transceiver = receiver + transmitter. PTT grounded = transmit.
  • Transverter = to another band. RF power amplifier = more output power.
  • Amplifier SSB/CW-FM switch = sets the amp for the mode.

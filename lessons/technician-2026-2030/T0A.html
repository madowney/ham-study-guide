Radio equipment runs on electricity, and some of it can hurt you. This lesson covers electrical safety at your station: wiring, fuses, batteries, grounding and lightning.

Why current is dangerous

Current flowing through your body can cause harm in all three ways: it heats tissue, disrupts the electrical signals of your cells (including your heart), and causes involuntary muscle contractions, so you may not be able to let go.

Household wiring

  • In U.S. three-wire 120 V AC cable, black is "hot", white is neutral, and green or bare is the safety ground.
  • A fuse or circuit breaker goes in series with the hot conductor only.
  • A fuse's purpose is to remove power if there's an overload.
  • Never replace a 5-amp fuse with a 20-amp fuse. The circuit could carry excessive current and start a fire before the bigger fuse blows.

Good habits that guard against shock (all of them): use three-wire cords and plugs, connect all equipment to a common safety ground, and fully discharge high-voltage capacitors before working inside equipment.

Power supplies and meters

  • Right after you turn a power supply off, its filter capacitors can still hold a dangerous charge.
  • When measuring high voltage, make sure the voltmeter and its leads are rated for that voltage.

Batteries

A 12-volt battery won't shock you, but it can deliver a huge current.

  • Shorting the terminals of an unprotected battery can cause burns, fire or an explosion.
  • Charging or discharging too fast can make it overheat or out-gas (release gas).

Lightning and grounding

  • Install a lightning arrester in the coax on a grounded panel near where feed lines enter the building.
  • Bond all outdoor ground rods together with heavy wire or conductive strap, so everything stays at the same voltage during a strike.

Key facts

  • Current through the body: heats tissue, disrupts cells, causes muscle contractions.
  • Black = hot. A fuse goes in series with the hot wire and removes power on overload.
  • Oversized fuse = fire risk.
  • Shock protection: three-wire cords, common ground, discharged capacitors.
  • Filter capacitors hold charge after turn-off. Use meters rated for the voltage.
  • Shorted battery: burns, fire, explosion. Fast charge or discharge: overheating or out-gassing.
  • Arrester on a grounded entry panel. Bond all ground rods together.

This lesson covers how a contact actually happens on the air: picking a frequency, calling someone, answering a call, and the basics of repeaters.

Simplex and repeaters

Simplex means both stations transmit and receive on the same frequency, radio to radio. The national FM simplex calling frequency on 2 meters is 146.520 MHz.

A repeater sits on a hill or tower, listens on one frequency (its input) and retransmits on another (its output). The repeater offset is the difference between the repeater's transmit and receive frequencies. Your radio handles it automatically once it's set up.

Memory hook: the bigger band number (2 meters) gets the smaller-looking offset, 600 kHz. The UHF band gets the big one, 5 MHz.

Band plans

The FCC says which modes are legal where. A band plan goes further: it's a voluntary guideline, agreed among hams, about which parts of a band to use for which modes or activities (repeaters here, simplex there, weak-signal work at the bottom). Following it keeps everyone out of each other's way.

Making contact

CQ means "calling any station."

  • Calling CQ without a repeater (on HF, or simplex): say "CQ" a few times, then "this is" and your call sign; pause to listen, and repeat if needed. "CQ CQ CQ, this is KF1ABC, KF1ABC, calling CQ and listening."
  • Answering a CQ: give their call sign first, then yours: "W1XYZ, this is KF1ABC."
  • Calling a specific station on a repeater: say their call sign, then yours. You don't need "break break" or "CQ."
  • Letting people know you're available on a repeater: say your call sign followed by "listening": "KF1ABC listening." Calling CQ on a repeater is unusual.

Key facts

  • Simplex = transmit and receive on the same frequency. 2 m calling frequency: 146.520 MHz.
  • Repeater offset = the difference between its transmit and receive frequencies.
  • Offsets: 2 m ±600 kHz; 70 cm ±5 MHz.
  • Band plan = a voluntary guideline beyond the FCC rules.
  • CQ = calling any station. Answering: their call, then yours.
  • On a repeater: "[their call], this is [your call]", or "[your call] listening."

Every radio is built from a small set of parts. This lesson covers the basic ones: resistors, capacitors, inductors, switches and batteries. You met their properties in the last lessons; now you'll meet the parts themselves.

The three basic components

ComponentWhat it doesBuilt as
ResistorOpposes the flow of currentCarbon or metal film, or wire
CapacitorStores energy in an electric fieldConductive surfaces separated by an insulator
InductorStores energy in a magnetic fieldA coil of wire

A potentiometer is an adjustable resistor. Turning its knob changes its resistance, which is why it's the classic volume control.

Switches

Switches are described by poles (how many separate circuits they control) and throws (how many positions each pole can connect to).

  • SPST (single-pole, single-throw): a plain on/off switch. One circuit, opened or closed. Component 3 in figure T-2 below is an SPST switch.
  • SPDT (single-pole, double-throw): one circuit switched between one of two other circuits, like a fork in the road.

Batteries

  • Rechargeable: nickel-metal hydride, nickel-cadmium, lithium-ion and lead-acid. All of them are rechargeable.
  • Not rechargeable: carbon-zinc, the cheap old-style flashlight battery.

Key facts

  • Resistor opposes current. A potentiometer is a variable resistance, used as a volume control.
  • Capacitor: electric field; conductive plates with an insulator between them.
  • Inductor: magnetic field; a coil of wire.
  • SPST = on/off (component 3 in T-2). SPDT = one circuit to either of two.
  • NiMH, Li-ion and lead-acid are rechargeable. Carbon-zinc is not.

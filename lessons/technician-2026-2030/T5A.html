This is where the electronics starts. If you've never studied electricity, don't worry: this lesson builds it from the ground up with a water analogy. Four words carry most of it: voltage, current, resistance and power.

The water analogy

QuantityWhat it isUnitLetter in formulas
VoltageThe electrical "pressure." A difference in voltage causes electrons to flow.volt (V)E
CurrentThe flow of electrons in a circuitampere (A), "amps"I
ResistanceOpposition to the flow. It opposes all current: DC, AC and RF.ohm (Ω)R
PowerThe rate at which energy is usedwatt (W)P

Watch the traps: power is measured in watts, not watt-hours (that's energy used over time). Current is measured in amperes, not volts.

Conductors and insulators

Metals conduct well because they have many free electrons that can move easily. Insulators like glass, plastic and ceramic hold their electrons tightly, so current can't flow. (Sea water, stainless steel and graphite all conduct.)

DC and AC

  • DC (direct current) flows one way, like from a battery.
  • AC (alternating current) alternates between the positive and negative directions, back and forth, like the power from a wall outlet. Radio signals are AC too, just very fast.
  • The number of complete back-and-forth cycles per second is the frequency, measured in hertz (Hz). Wall power in the U.S. is 60 Hz; the 2-meter band is about 146,000,000 Hz.

Key facts

  • Current = the flow of electrons, measured in amperes. A voltage difference causes it.
  • Power = the rate of energy use, measured in watts.
  • Resistance opposes all current (DC, AC, RF), measured in ohms.
  • Metals conduct because they have free electrons. Glass insulates.
  • AC alternates positive and negative. Cycles per second = frequency, in hertz.

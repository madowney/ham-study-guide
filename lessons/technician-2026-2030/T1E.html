Every transmitting ham station needs a responsible person: the control operator. This lesson covers who that is, where they are, and the three ways a station can be controlled.

The control operator

Part 97 defines a control operator as an amateur operator designated by the station's licensee to be responsible for the transmissions and for following the FCC rules. It isn't necessarily the person talking, and it isn't necessarily the person who owns the station.

  • A station may never transmit without a control operator. Not even a repeater.
  • The station licensee (owner) designates the control operator.
  • If someone else is the control operator, both the control operator and the station licensee are responsible.

Whose privileges apply?

The control operator's license class determines which frequencies the station may use. Not the license on the wall, and not the best license in the room. So a Technician may never be the control operator on an Amateur Extra-only band segment (except in an emergency), even at an Extra's station or a club station.

Satellites follow the same logic: anyone allowed to transmit on the satellite's uplink frequency may be the control operator for that contact.

The control point

The control point is wherever the control operator is doing the controlling. It isn't where the antenna or the transmitter is.

Any amateur station may be remotely controlled, not just repeaters. Watch the decoys: a software-defined radio, a radio-controlled model plane, and bouncing signals off the Moon are not examples of remote control.

Key facts

  • Control operator: designated by the licensee, responsible for compliance.
  • Never transmit without a control operator.
  • The licensee designates; the licensee and the control operator are both responsible.
  • Frequency privileges = the control operator's license class.
  • Control point = where the control operator is.
  • Automatic control: a repeater. Remote control: over the internet. Any station may be remote.

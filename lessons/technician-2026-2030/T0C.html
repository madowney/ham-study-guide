Radio waves are energy, and at high enough levels they can heat your body. This lesson explains what RF exposure is (and isn't), what affects it, and your responsibility as a station licensee.

What kind of radiation is it?

Radio signals are non-ionizing radiation. Unlike X-rays or radioactivity, RF doesn't have enough energy to cause chemical changes in cells or damage DNA. Its main effect is heating. "Not ionizing" doesn't mean "perfectly safe," though; strong RF can still cause harm.

Touching an antenna while it's transmitting can give you an RF burn to your skin.

What affects exposure

RF exposure depends on all of these: the frequency and power, your distance from the antenna, and the antenna's radiation pattern.

The limits vary with frequency because the body absorbs more RF at some frequencies than at others. The body absorbs most efficiently around 30 to 300 MHz, so that range has the lowest (strictest) limit. Of the exam's choices, that's 50 MHz.

Duty cycle

Duty cycle is the percentage of time a transmitter is transmitting. It matters because it affects your average exposure. If the duty cycle drops from 100% to 50%, the allowable power density doubles (it increases by a factor of 2), because you're only exposed half the time.

Staying in compliance

  • The station licensee is responsible for making sure nobody is exposed above the FCC limits.
  • You can evaluate your station in any of these ways: calculation using FCC OET Bulletin 65, computer modeling, or measurement with calibrated equipment.
  • Re-evaluate whenever you change anything in the transmitter or antenna system.
  • To reduce exposure, move antennas farther from people.

Key facts

  • RF is non-ionizing: it can't damage DNA. The main risk is heating. Touching a transmitting antenna can cause an RF burn.
  • Exposure depends on frequency, power, distance and pattern.
  • Limits vary because absorption varies with frequency. The strictest limit among the choices: 50 MHz.
  • Duty cycle = % of time transmitting. It affects average exposure; at 50% duty cycle the allowable density doubles.
  • The licensee is responsible. Evaluate by OET 65, modeling or measurement. Re-evaluate after changes.
  • Relocating antennas reduces exposure.

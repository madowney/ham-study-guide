Resistance isn't the only thing that affects electricity. Some components store energy, and that matters a lot at radio frequencies. This lesson also covers the power formula, which works a lot like Ohm's law.

Storing energy

PropertyStores energy in…UnitComponent
Capacitancean electric fieldfarad (F)capacitor
Inductancea magnetic fieldhenry (H)inductor (coil)

Memory hook: Capacitance holds Charge, in an electric field. An inductor is a coil of wire, and coils make magnets.

Impedance

Impedance is the opposition to AC current flow. It's resistance plus the effect of capacitance and inductance, which act differently depending on frequency. Like resistance, it's measured in ohms. You'll meet it again with antennas and feed lines, which are typically 50 ohms.

Writing the units correctly

Capitalization matters: kHz for kilohertz (lower-case k, capital H), MHz for megahertz (capital M, because lower-case m means "milli").

The power formula

P = I × E

Power (watts) equals current (amps) times voltage (volts). Same triangle trick as Ohm's law: P on top, I and E on the bottom.

Key facts

  • Capacitance: electric field, farads. Inductance: magnetic field, henrys.
  • Impedance = opposition to AC, measured in ohms.
  • kHz and MHz are the correct abbreviations.
  • P = I × E. 13.8 V × 10 A = 138 W. 12 V × 2.5 A = 30 W. 120 W ÷ 12 V = 10 A.

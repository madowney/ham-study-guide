Semiconductors are the parts that made modern radio possible: diodes and transistors. You don't need the physics, just what each part does and the names of its leads.

Diodes: one-way valves

A diode lets current flow in only one direction. Its two leads (electrodes) are the anode and the cathode. On the package, the cathode end is usually marked with a stripe.

A diode "costs" a little voltage when current flows through it, called the forward voltage drop. It's normal, not a defect, and it's lower in some diode types than in others.

An LED (light-emitting diode) gives off light when forward current flows through it.

Transistors: switches and amplifiers

A transistor uses a small signal to control a bigger one. That lets it act as an electronic switch or an amplifier. Transistors can provide power gain; resistors and transformers can't.

A common type has three regions of semiconductor material. The two main kinds:

TypeLeads
Bipolar junction transistor (BJT)Emitter, base, collector
Field-effect transistor (FET)Gate, drain, source

Memory hook: an FET is like a garden: a gate and a drain that water flows from at the source.

Gain

An amplifier's gain compares its output to its input. It can be measured as voltage, current or power, so all three exam answers are correct.

Key facts

  • Diode: one direction only; anode and cathode; the stripe marks the cathode.
  • Forward voltage drop is normal and varies by diode type.
  • An LED lights up from forward current.
  • Transistor: an electronic switch with power gain; can have three semiconductor regions.
  • BJT: emitter, base, collector. FET (field effect transistor): gate, drain, source.
  • Gain = output relative to input (voltage, current or power).

This lesson covers how basic parts combine into useful functions: turning AC into DC, switching with electricity, regulating voltage, and tuning to a frequency.

Inside a power supply

Here's how a power supply turns wall power into steady DC, following figure T-2 left to right:

  1. A transformer changes 120 V AC to a lower AC voltage.
  2. A rectifier (built from diodes) changes AC into a varying DC: current now flows one way, but it's bumpy.
  3. A capacitor smooths out the bumps.
  4. A regulator controls the amount of voltage, holding it steady.

Other building blocks

  • Relay: an electrically controlled switch. A small current flips a switch that can carry a big one.
  • Shielded wire: wire wrapped in a metal shield, to prevent unwanted signals from coupling into or out of it.
  • Meter: displays an electrical quantity as a number.
  • LED: commonly used as an indicator light.
  • Integrated circuit (IC): many semiconductors and other parts combined in one package, a "chip."
  • In figure T-1, the transistor's job is to control the flow of current through the lamp.

Resonant (tuned) circuits

Combine an inductor and a capacitor, in series or in parallel, and you get a resonant circuit. It responds strongly to one particular frequency. This is how radios tune to a station.

"LC" circuit: L is the symbol for inductance, C for capacitance. L plus C equals tuned.

Key facts

  • Transformer: 120 V AC to a lower AC voltage. Rectifier: AC to varying DC. Regulator: controls the voltage.
  • Relay = an electrically controlled switch. Shielded wire prevents unwanted coupling.
  • Meter = a numeric display. LED = an indicator. IC = many parts in one package.
  • Inductor + capacitor (series or parallel) = resonant circuit.

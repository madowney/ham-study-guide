The antenna system has to be matched to your radio for your power to go out instead of bouncing back. This lesson covers SWR, the tools that measure it, and how to keep coax cable healthy.

SWR: how well things match

Radios, coax and antennas are designed around 50 ohms of impedance. When the antenna doesn't match the feed line, some power reflects back toward the radio. SWR (standing wave ratio) measures how good the match is:

  • 1:1 is a perfect match.
  • Higher numbers mean a worse match. 4:1 indicates an impedance mismatch.

Modern solid-state transmitters cut back their power when SWR gets high, to protect their output transistors from damage.

Power that's lost in a feed line is turned into heat.

Test equipment

  • Directional wattmeter: measures forward and reflected power, so it can determine SWR.
  • Antenna analyzer: tells you whether an antenna is resonant at the frequency you want.
  • Dummy load: a 50-ohm non-inductive resistor on a heat sink. It soaks up your transmitter's power as heat so you can test without transmitting over the air.

Coaxial cable care

  • The number one killer of coax is moisture getting inside.
  • The outer jacket should resist ultraviolet light, because sunlight damages the jacket and lets water in.
  • Foam-dielectric coax has less loss per foot than solid-dielectric coax.

Key facts

  • SWR 1:1 = perfect match. 4:1 = mismatch.
  • Radios cut power at high SWR to protect their output transistors.
  • Feed line loss becomes heat.
  • Directional wattmeter measures SWR. Antenna analyzer checks resonance.
  • Dummy load: a 50-ohm non-inductive resistor on a heat sink; for testing off the air.
  • Coax fails from moisture. A UV-resistant jacket keeps water out. Foam = lower loss.

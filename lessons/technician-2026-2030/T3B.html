Radio is just energy traveling through space as a wave. Three ideas from this lesson will come up again and again: how fast radio waves travel, how long each wave is, and how the bands are named.

What a radio wave is made of

A radio wave is an electromagnetic wave. It has two parts traveling together: an electric field and a magnetic field. The two fields always sit at right angles to each other, like the floor and a wall.

The direction of the electric field sets the wave's polarization. If the electric field goes up and down, the wave is vertically polarized; side to side, horizontally polarized. A vertical antenna sends out a vertically polarized wave.

Light is also an electromagnetic wave, just at a much higher frequency. That's why radio waves travel at the speed of light: about 300,000,000 meters per second in free space. Every radio frequency travels at that same speed. Microwaves aren't faster than VHF, and VHF isn't faster than HF.

Frequency and wavelength

The frequency is how many waves pass a point each second, measured in hertz (Hz). One megahertz (MHz) is a million waves per second. The wavelength is the distance from one wave crest to the next.

The speed is fixed, so if more waves pass each second, each one must be shorter. As frequency goes up, wavelength gets shorter.

You need one formula. Memorize it now, because it shows up all over the exam:

wavelength (meters) = 300 ÷ frequency (MHz)

For example, 146 MHz: 300 ÷ 146 ≈ 2 meters. 28 MHz: 300 ÷ 28 ≈ 10.7, so about 10 meters. 446 MHz: 300 ÷ 446 ≈ 0.67 meters, or 70 centimeters.

Watch the trap. The wrong answers flip it ("frequency divided by 300") or use hertz instead of megahertz. It's always 300 divided by MHz.

Naming the bands

Hams name bands by their approximate wavelength in meters: the "2-meter band" (144–148 MHz), the "10-meter band" (28–29.7 MHz), the "70-centimeter band" (420–450 MHz). You'll hear band names more often than frequencies.

Ranges of frequencies also have letter names. Each one covers a factor of ten:

NameRangeHam bands in it
HF (high frequency)3 to 30 MHz80, 40, 20, 15, 10 meters…
VHF (very high)30 to 300 MHz6 meters, 2 meters, 1.25 meters
UHF (ultra high)300 to 3000 MHz70 cm, 33 cm, 23 cm

A memory hook: HF starts at 3, VHF at 30, UHF at 300 (all in MHz). Each range ends where the next one starts.

Key facts

  • A radio wave = electric field + magnetic field, at right angles to each other.
  • Polarization = the direction of the electric field.
  • Speed in free space = speed of light ≈ 300,000,000 m/s, the same for every frequency.
  • Higher frequency means shorter wavelength. Wavelength (m) = 300 ÷ MHz.
  • Bands are named by approximate wavelength in meters.
  • HF 3–30 MHz · VHF 30–300 MHz · UHF 300–3000 MHz.

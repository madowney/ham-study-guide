Ohm's law is the most useful formula in electronics, and it's worth a dozen exam questions. It links voltage, current and resistance, and once you can use it, every one of those questions is just arithmetic.

Ohm's law

E = I × R

E is voltage (volts), I is current (amps), R is resistance (ohms). Rearranged, it gives all three formulas the exam asks for:

  • Current: I = E ÷ R
  • Voltage: E = I × R
  • Resistance: R = E ÷ I

Cover I: you see E over R, so I = E ÷ R. Cover E: you see I next to R, so E = I × R. Cover R: you see E over I, so R = E ÷ I.

Series and parallel

  • Series: everything is in one line, so the same current flows through every component.
  • Parallel: components sit side by side across the same two points, so each one has the same voltage across it.

Key facts

  • I = E ÷ R · E = I × R · R = E ÷ I. (E = voltage, I = current.)
  • Use the triangle: E on top, I and R on the bottom.
  • Series: same current. Parallel: same voltage.

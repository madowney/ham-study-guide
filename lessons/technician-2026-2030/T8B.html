Yes, a Technician can talk through satellites. Ham satellites relay signals from one part of the world to another, and the ISS carries a ham station too. This lesson covers how satellite operating works.

Satellite basics

  • LEO means Low Earth Orbit. These satellites circle the planet in about 100 minutes, so each pass over you lasts only about 10 minutes.
  • The uplink is the frequency you transmit to the satellite; the downlink is where it transmits back.
  • U/V mode means the uplink is on 70 centimeters (UHF) and the downlink is on 2 meters (VHF). The first letter is the uplink.
  • Satellites use all sorts of modes: SSB, FM, CW and data.

Beacons and telemetry

A satellite beacon is a transmission from the satellite containing status information. That telemetry reports the satellite's health and status: battery voltage, temperatures and so on. Anyone may receive it; it's not secret.

Tracking a satellite

A tracking program needs the satellite's orbit, described by its Keplerian elements. From them it provides all of these:

  • A real-time map of where the satellite is
  • When a pass starts, peaks and ends, and where to point your antenna (azimuth and elevation)
  • The frequency you'll actually hear, corrected for Doppler shift

Doppler shift is the change in frequency caused by the satellite moving relative to you, like a passing siren's pitch dropping. The frequency is higher as the satellite approaches and lower as it moves away.

Spin fading is the signal fading in and out because the satellite and its antennas are rotating.

Operating courtesy

Many satellites share their capacity among everyone using them. If you transmit with too much power, you hog it and block access for other users. The right level: your signal on the downlink should be about as strong as the satellite's beacon.

Key facts

  • LEO = Low Earth Orbit, a period of about 100 minutes.
  • U/V = uplink on 70 cm, downlink on 2 m.
  • Beacon = status info; telemetry = health and status; anyone can receive it.
  • Tracking programs take Keplerian elements and give maps, pass times and pointing, and Doppler.
  • Doppler = frequency change from relative motion. Spin fading = the satellite rotating.
  • Too much power blocks others. Match your downlink to the beacon's strength.
  • Satellites use SSB, FM, CW and data.

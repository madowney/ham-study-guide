Antennas go up high, and that brings two big dangers: falling and power lines. This lesson covers putting up antennas and towers safely.

Power lines

Power lines are the deadliest hazard in antenna work.

  • Before putting up a tower or antenna, look for overhead power lines and stay clear.
  • Minimum distance: far enough that if the antenna falls, no part of it can come within 10 feet of the power wires.
  • Never attach an antenna to a utility pole: it could contact high-voltage lines.

Climbing

  • Climbing requires all of these: proper training, an approved climbing harness, and staying tied off to the tower at all times.
  • It is never safe to climb without a helper or observer.
  • A crank-up tower must not be climbed unless it's fully retracted or mechanical safety locks are installed.
  • A safety wire through a turnbuckle (the tensioner on a guy wire) keeps vibration from loosening it.

Grounding a tower

  • Proper grounding: a separate 8-foot ground rod for each tower leg, bonded to the tower and to each other.
  • Lightning ground wires should be short and direct, with no sharp bends. Lightning doesn't like turning corners.
  • Grounding requirements are set by local electrical codes, not the FCC.

Key facts

  • Stay clear of power lines: a falling antenna must stay 10 feet or more away. Never use utility poles.
  • Climbing: training, harness, tie-off. Never climb alone.
  • Crank-up tower: retracted or locked before climbing.
  • Turnbuckle safety wire prevents loosening from vibration.
  • Tower ground: an 8-foot rod per leg, all bonded. Short, direct, no sharp bends.
  • Grounding rules come from local electrical codes.

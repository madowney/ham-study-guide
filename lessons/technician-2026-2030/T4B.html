This lesson covers the knobs and settings on a modern radio: microphone gain, squelch, filters, the clarifier, and programming digital radios.

Tuning and listening

  • Set the frequency with the keypad or the VFO knob (the big tuning dial).
  • Scanning tunes through a range of frequencies to check for activity.
  • To hear a weak FM signal, open the squelch all the way: set it so the audio is on all the time. You'll hear hiss, but you won't miss a weak signal.
  • An FM signal received slightly off frequency sounds distorted.

Single sideband (SSB) controls

  • Too much microphone gain on SSB makes your transmitted audio distorted.
  • If someone's voice sounds too high or too low (like a chipmunk or a bear), they're slightly off frequency. Fix it with the RIT (receiver incremental tuning), also called the clarifier. It shifts only your receive frequency.

Filters

A good radio lets you choose its receive filter bandwidth. Matching the bandwidth to the mode reduces noise and interference, because a wider filter lets in more noise without more signal.

ModeTypical filter
CW (Morse)About 500 Hz
SSB voiceAbout 2400 Hz (best signal-to-noise for SSB)
FM voiceMuch wider, 10 to 15 kHz

Digital voice radios

  • DMR code plug: the configuration data loaded onto your radio so it can access repeaters and talkgroups. It's a file, not a physical plug.
  • To select a group on DMR, enter the group's ID code.
  • D-STAR radios must have your call sign programmed in before you transmit.

Key facts

  • Set frequency with the keypad or VFO knob. Scanning checks a range for activity.
  • Weak FM: squelch fully open. FM off frequency = distorted.
  • Too much mic gain = distorted audio. Voice pitch wrong on SSB: RIT/clarifier.
  • Filter choice reduces noise. SSB: 2400 Hz.
  • DMR code plug = configuration data. Select a group by its ID. D-STAR needs your call sign programmed.

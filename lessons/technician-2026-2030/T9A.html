The antenna is where electricity becomes a radio wave. This lesson covers the common antenna types, how length relates to frequency, and what "gain" really means.

Polarization and gain

  • An antenna's polarization is described by the orientation of its electric field. A vertical antenna gives vertical polarization.
  • Antenna gain is the increase in signal strength in a specific direction compared to a reference antenna. An antenna can't create power; gain comes from concentrating the signal in one direction instead of spreading it everywhere.
  • A beam antenna concentrates signals in one direction. The Yagi, with several elements in a row like a TV antenna, has the greatest gain of the common types.

The dipole

A half-wave dipole is a wire half a wavelength long, fed in the middle. It's the basic reference antenna.

  • It radiates strongest broadside, perpendicular to the wire, and weakest off its ends.
  • Shortening an antenna raises its resonant frequency, because a shorter wave means a higher frequency. Lengthening it lowers the frequency.

Verticals and length

  • A quarter-wave vertical on 2 meters is about 19 inches long. That's why 19-inch antennas are so common: it's a resonant quarter-wave. (A quarter of 2 meters is 50 cm, or about 19.5 inches.)
  • A 5/8-wave whip has more gain than a 1/4-wave, because it sends more signal toward the horizon.

Short antennas and loading

  • Loading means making an antenna act longer electrically than it is physically, by adding inductors (coils) to it. That's why mobile HF antennas have a coil in them.
  • The short, flexible "rubber duck" on a handheld is convenient but has low efficiency compared with a full quarter-wave.
  • Using a handheld inside a car: the metal body shields the signal, reducing strength. Use an outside antenna.

Key facts

  • Polarization = the electric field's orientation. Gain = more signal in one direction vs. a reference.
  • Beam = concentrates in one direction. Yagi = the highest gain.
  • Dipole radiates strongest broadside. Shorter = higher resonant frequency.
  • 2 m quarter-wave ≈ 19 inches. 5/8-wave beats 1/4-wave for gain.
  • Loading = adding inductors to lengthen electrically. Rubber duck = low efficiency. A car body shields a handheld.

A grab bag of rules: how tall your antenna can be, what beacons are, which one-way transmissions are allowed, and who sets the rules where Part 97 is silent.

Antennas: height and local rules

  • An antenna structure up to 200 feet above ground needs no FAA notification or FCC registration, as long as it isn't near a public-use airport. Above 200 feet, it does.
  • State and local governments can regulate antenna structures, but with limits. This federal policy is known as PRB-1. Amateur communications must be reasonably accommodated, and the regulations must be the minimum practical to achieve the local government's legitimate purpose.

Beacons

A beacon is an unattended station that transmits continuously so others can check conditions. Its purpose under FCC rules is observation of propagation and reception. If you hear a beacon a thousand miles away, you know that path is open.

  • Automatically controlled HF beacons are allowed only from 28.20 to 28.30 MHz.
  • Power limit: 100 watts PEP output.
  • Only one beacon per band from the same station location.

One-way transmissions

Amateur radio is mostly two-way. The exam asks about two exceptions:

  • Morse code practice: transmissions to help people learn the International Morse code are allowed.
  • Weather and propagation forecasts: occasional retransmission of these from US government stations is permitted for all amateur stations.

The wrong answers are worth knowing as traps. Unidentified test transmissions are never allowed, however short. Encrypted messages are prohibited. Regularly advertising equipment for sale is not allowed. And only certain stations, such as repeaters, may automatically retransmit other amateur signals.

Language and foreign contacts

  • Abbreviations and procedural signals (like "73" or "QTH") are fine if they don't obscure the meaning of a message.
  • You may contact amateurs in any country except those whose governments have told the ITU they object. No third-party agreement is needed just to chat with another ham.

Good amateur practice

For anything Part 97 doesn't spell out, the FCC decides what counts as "good engineering and good amateur practice." It isn't the control operator, the IEEE or the ITU.

Key facts

  • Antenna over 200 feet (or near an airport): FAA notification and FCC registration.
  • PRB-1: local rules must reasonably accommodate amateur radio, at the minimum practical level.
  • Beacons: for observing propagation. 28.20–28.30 MHz on HF, 100 W PEP max, one per band per location.
  • OK one-way: Morse code practice, occasional government weather and propagation forecasts.
  • Abbreviations are fine if the meaning stays clear.
  • Contacts are allowed with any country unless it has objected to the ITU.
  • The FCC defines "good amateur practice."

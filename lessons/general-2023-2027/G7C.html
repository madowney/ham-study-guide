This lesson looks inside transmitters and receivers: how an SSB signal is made and decoded, how filters are described, and what software-defined radios do differently.

Making and receiving SSB

  1. A balanced modulator combines your voice with a carrier and cancels the carrier. Its output is double-sideband RF.
  2. A filter then selects one of the two sidebands. That's SSB.
  3. On the receiving end, a product detector extracts the voice from the SSB signal.

Describing filters

  • Insertion loss: the filter's attenuation inside its passband, the price of putting it in the line.
  • Ultimate rejection: its maximum ability to reject signals outside the passband.
  • Bandwidth of a band-pass filter: measured between the upper and lower half-power points.
  • Cutoff frequency: for a low-pass filter, the frequency above which output power is less than half the input.

Digital radio building blocks

  • A DSP filter's advantage over an analog one: a wide range of bandwidths and shapes, all done in software.
  • A direct digital synthesizer (DDS) gives a variable output frequency with the stability of a crystal oscillator.
  • Software-defined radios do filtering, detection and modulation in software (all of these).
  • SDRs use I and Q signals, which are 90 degrees apart. With I-Q, any type of modulation can be created with the right processing.

Odds and ends

  • An impedance-matching transformer at a transmitter's output presents the right impedance to both the transmitter and the feed line.
  • Receiver sensitivity is affected by all of these: input amplifier gain, demodulator bandwidth and input noise figure.

Key facts

  • Balanced modulator: double-sideband output. A filter selects one sideband. Product detector: demodulates SSB.
  • Insertion loss: loss inside the passband. Ultimate rejection: maximum rejection outside it.
  • Bandwidth: between the half-power points. Cutoff frequency: where output falls below half power.
  • DSP: many filter shapes. DDS: variable frequency with crystal stability.
  • SDR: filtering, detection and modulation in software. I and Q are 90° apart and can make any modulation.
  • Receiver sensitivity: gain, bandwidth, noise figure (all of these).

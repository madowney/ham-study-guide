This lesson covers how digital modes encode text, catch errors, and show up on a waterfall display. It's the vocabulary behind FT8, PSK31, RTTY and packet.

Codes

  • Baudot (used by RTTY): a 5-bit code with extra start and stop bits.
  • Varicode (used by PSK31): common characters get short codes, rare ones long codes. Upper-case letters use longer codes, so they slow transmission down. Type in lower case.
  • The two tones of an FSK signal are called mark and space.

Catching errors

  • Forward error correction (FEC) sends redundant information along with the data, so the receiver can fix errors on its own.
  • ARQ modes ask for repeats instead. A NAK reply means "please retransmit that packet."
  • If too many retries fail, the connection is dropped.
  • In a packet radio frame, the header holds the routing and handling information.
  • In a mesh network, if one node fails, a packet can still get through by another node.

Weak-signal modes

  • FT8 can receive signals with very low signal-to-noise ratios, often below the noise you can hear.
  • An FT8 report of +3 means a signal-to-noise ratio equivalent to +3 dB in a 2.5 kHz bandwidth.
  • WSPR is a low-power beacon mode for checking HF propagation.

Reading a waterfall

  • A waterfall shows frequency horizontally, signal strength as brightness, and time vertically.
  • Vertical lines on either side of a data or RTTY signal mean overmodulation.

Digital voice

The digital voice modes are DMR, D-STAR and System Fusion. FT8, WSPR and Winlink are data modes, not voice.

Key facts

  • Baudot: 5 bits plus start and stop. PSK31 uses Varicode; capitals are slower. FSK tones: mark and space.
  • FEC: redundant data. ARQ NAK: retransmit. Too many retries: connection dropped. Packet header: routing.
  • Mesh: traffic can route around a failed node.
  • FT8 works at very low SNR. +3 = +3 dB SNR in 2.5 kHz. WSPR: a propagation beacon.
  • Waterfall: frequency across, time down, strength as brightness. Side lines mean overmodulation.
  • Digital voice: DMR, D-STAR, System Fusion.

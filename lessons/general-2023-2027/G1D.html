This lesson covers what happens around the exam itself: who runs it, what your pass certificate lets you do the same day, and how licenses work when you operate a station from far away.

Volunteer Examiners

Amateur exams are given by Volunteer Examiners (VEs), not by the FCC.

  • VEs are accredited by a Volunteer Examiner Coordinator (VEC), such as the ARRL VEC or W5YI. Not by the FCC directly.
  • You must be at least 18 years old to be a VE.
  • A non-US citizen can be a VE if they hold an FCC amateur license of General class or above.
  • A General-class VE may give the Technician exam only. Higher exams need higher-class examiners.
  • Every exam session must be observed by at least three VEs of General class or higher.

Your CSCE: operating before the FCC catches up

When you pass, the VEs hand you a Certificate of Successful Completion of Examination (CSCE). It takes a few days for the upgrade to show up in the FCC database.

  • A Technician holding a CSCE for General may operate on any General or Technician band segment right away.
  • Until the upgrade appears in the database, add "AG" after your call sign whenever you use General frequency privileges. For example: "KF0ABC stroke AG". On Technician frequencies, you don't need it.
  • A CSCE is good for exam element credit for 365 days.

You plan to take both exams in one sitting. If you pass both, you'll walk out with a CSCE and can operate on General frequencies, signing /AG, until your new license shows up.

Expired licenses

  • Anyone who can show they once held a General, Advanced or Extra license that wasn't revoked gets partial exam credit.
  • If the license expired and the two-year grace period has passed, to get a General license again you must show proof of the old license and pass the current Element 2 (Technician) exam.

Remote operation

You can run a station over the internet from somewhere else. The rule is that the station's location decides the rules.

  • US station, you're abroad: you need a US operator/primary station license. No special permit is needed.
  • Station in South America, you're in the US: only that country's regulations apply.

Key facts

  • VEs: accredited by a VEC, at least 18 years old, and three VEs (General or higher) observe each exam.
  • A General VE can give the Technician exam only.
  • A non-citizen VE needs an FCC General or higher license.
  • CSCE: operate on General segments right away. Add "AG" when you use General privileges. Valid 365 days.
  • Old General, Advanced or Extra license: partial credit. Past the grace period: show proof and pass Element 2.
  • Remote operation: the station's country's rules apply. A US station needs a US license.

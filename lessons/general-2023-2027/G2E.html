Today a lot of HF activity is computers talking through radios. You plug the radio into a laptop, and software turns text into tones. This lesson covers the modes you'll meet: FT8, RTTY, and email-over-radio systems like Winlink.

How it works: AFSK

Most digital modes use AFSK (audio frequency-shift keying). The computer makes audio tones and feeds them into an SSB transmitter, like whistling into the microphone. The sideband you choose matters:

FT8, FT4, JT65, JT9USB
RTTY via AFSKLSB (by tradition)

FT8

FT8 is the most popular HF digital mode. It's very sensitive and transmits in fixed 15-second time slots.

  • It requires your computer's clock to be accurate to within about 1 second, because both stations must agree on when each slot starts. No special hardware modem is needed.
  • On 20 meters it lives around 14.074 to 14.077 MHz. Most digital activity on 20 meters is between 14.070 and 14.100 MHz.
  • To answer a CQ, find a clear frequency in the alternate time slot from the calling station. You transmit while it listens.

RTTY

Radioteletype shifts between two tones, "mark" and "space." On amateur HF the usual shift is 170 Hz. If a RTTY signal seems tuned in but won't decode, all of these could be the cause: mark and space reversed, the wrong baud rate, or the wrong sideband.

Email over radio: Winlink, PACTOR and VARA

  • Winlink is an amateur radio network for sending and receiving email. It's a form of packet radio and works on both VHF and HF, so all those choices are correct.
  • A Winlink Remote Message Server is also called a gateway. To reach one, transmit a connect message on its published frequency.
  • VARA is a digital protocol used with Winlink. PACTOR is another.
  • PACTOR links are between exactly two stations. You can't join a contact already in progress.
  • When another signal interferes with PACTOR or VARA, you can see all of these: frequent retries or timeouts, long pauses, or failure to connect.

Mesh networks

AREDN (Amateur Radio Emergency Data Network) is a mesh network. Its job is to provide high-speed data during emergencies or community events.

Key facts

  • FT8, FT4, JT65, JT9: USB. RTTY AFSK: LSB. RTTY shift: 170 Hz.
  • FT8 needs the computer clock within about 1 second. It's at about 14.074–14.077 MHz. Answer in the alternate time slot.
  • 20 m digital: mostly 14.070–14.100 MHz.
  • RTTY won't decode: reversed mark and space, wrong baud rate or wrong sideband (all of these).
  • Winlink: email over radio, a form of packet, VHF and HF (all of these). Its servers are called gateways. Connect on the published frequency.
  • VARA: a protocol used with Winlink. PACTOR: two stations only.
  • Interference with PACTOR or VARA: retries, pauses, failed connections (all of these).
  • AREDN: high-speed data for emergencies and events.

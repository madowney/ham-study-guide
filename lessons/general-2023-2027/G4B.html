This lesson covers test equipment: oscilloscopes, meters and antenna analyzers. For each, learn what it measures and when you'd reach for it.

Oscilloscope

An oscilloscope draws a voltage over time on a screen. It has horizontal and vertical channel amplifiers.

  • Its advantage over a digital voltmeter: it can show complex waveforms.
  • It's the best instrument for checking a CW keying waveform, to see clean, rounded edges instead of clicks.
  • To view a transmitted signal's RF envelope, feed the attenuated RF output of the transmitter into the vertical input.

Two-tone test

Feed the SSB transmitter two audio tones that aren't harmonically related, then look at the output on a scope. The test checks linearity: whether the amplifier reproduces its input faithfully.

Meters

  • Voltmeters have high input impedance so they draw almost no current from the circuit they're measuring.
  • Digital multimeter advantage: higher precision.
  • Analog multimeter advantage: better when adjusting for a maximum or minimum. It's easier to watch a needle peak than flickering digits.
  • A directional wattmeter measures forward and reflected power, so it can tell you SWR.

Antenna analyzer

An antenna analyzer is a small, low-power signal source with an SWR and impedance bridge built in.

  • To measure SWR, connect it to the antenna and feed line. No transmitter or receiver is involved.
  • It can also measure the impedance of coaxial cable.
  • Strong signals from nearby transmitters can add received power that throws off its SWR readings.

Key facts

  • Oscilloscope: horizontal and vertical amplifiers. It shows complex waveforms and is best for checking CW keying.
  • RF envelope: put the attenuated transmitter output on the vertical input.
  • Two-tone test: two non-harmonically related tones; it checks linearity.
  • High voltmeter impedance: less loading. Digital: precision. Analog: finding peaks and dips.
  • Directional wattmeter: SWR.
  • Antenna analyzer: connect the antenna and feed line. It measures coax impedance, and nearby transmitters can upset it.

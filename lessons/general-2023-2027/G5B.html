Power

In a parallel circuit, the total current equals the sum of the currents through each branch.

Peak, peak-to-peak and RMS

  • RMS is the AC value that produces the same heating in a resistor as the same DC voltage. Wall power, 120 V, is an RMS figure.
  • RMS = peak × 0.707. Peak = RMS × 1.414. Peak-to-peak = 2 × peak.
  • 17 V peak × 0.707 = 12 V RMS.
  • 120 V RMS × 1.414 = 169.7 V peak, × 2 = 339.4 V peak-to-peak.

PEP from a voltage reading

To get PEP from a peak-to-peak reading: halve it to get peak, multiply by 0.707 for RMS, then use P = E² ÷ R.

  • 200 V p-p → 100 V peak → 70.7 V RMS → 70.7² ÷ 50 = 100 W
  • 500 V p-p → 250 V peak → 176.8 V RMS → 176.8² ÷ 50 = 625 W

An unmodulated carrier is a steady signal, so its peak power equals its average power. The PEP-to-average ratio is 1.00, and 1060 W average means 1060 W PEP.

Decibels

  • 3 dB = a factor of two in power (double or half).
  • 1 dB of loss = 20.6 percent of the power lost.

Key facts

  • P = E × I = E² ÷ R = I² × R. Examples: 2.4 W, 200 W, 61 mW. 1200 W in 50 Ω = 245 V RMS.
  • Parallel circuit: the total current is the sum of the branch currents.
  • RMS = same heating as DC. RMS = 0.707 × peak. Peak-to-peak = 2 × peak.
  • 17 V peak = 12 V RMS. 120 V RMS = 339.4 V peak-to-peak.
  • PEP into 50 Ω: 200 V p-p = 100 W, 500 V p-p = 625 W.
  • Unmodulated carrier: PEP/average = 1.00.
  • 3 dB = 2× power. 1 dB loss = 20.6%.

There are many specialty antennas, and each one exists to solve a particular problem. For each, learn what it's for, and the question answers itself.

A tour of specialty antennas

Trade-offs

  • The disadvantage of a multiband antenna is poor harmonic rejection. If your transmitter puts out a harmonic on another band the antenna covers, it radiates that too.
  • Vertically stacking horizontal Yagis narrows the main lobe in elevation, concentrating power at low angles for distance.

Key facts

  • NVIS on 40 m: a horizontal dipole 1/10 to 1/4 wavelength high.
  • Inverted V: a dipole on one central support. End-fed half-wave: very high impedance.
  • Traps: multiband. Multiband antennas: poor harmonic rejection.
  • Log-periodic: lengths and spacing vary logarithmically, giving wide bandwidth.
  • Halo: omnidirectional in its plane. Screwdriver: varies base inductance.
  • Beverage: directional receiving on MF and low HF. Small loop: nulls broadside.
  • Stacked Yagis: narrower elevation lobe.

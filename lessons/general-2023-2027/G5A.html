With DC, only resistance limits current. With AC, coils and capacitors push back too, and how hard they push depends on frequency. That push is called reactance. It's the key to how radios tune.

Reactance

Reactance is opposition to the flow of alternating current caused by capacitance or inductance. So the opposition to AC in an inductor is reactance, and the opposition to AC in a capacitor is reactance too.

  • Its symbol is X.
  • It's measured in ohms, like resistance.

The two kinds go in opposite directions as frequency changes:

  • Inductor: as frequency increases, its reactance increases. A coil resists fast changes.
  • Capacitor: as frequency increases, its reactance decreases. High frequencies pass through a capacitor easily.

Reactance depends on frequency, not on the amplitude (size) of the AC. Answers that mention amplitude are wrong.

Resonance

At one frequency, the inductive and capacitive reactance are equal. That's resonance, and there the two reactances cancel. In a series LC circuit, the impedance at resonance is very low, leaving only resistance. This is how a radio picks out one frequency.

Impedance and admittance

  • Impedance (Z) is the total opposition to AC: resistance plus reactance. It's the ratio of voltage to current, just like Ohm's law.
  • Admittance is the inverse of impedance: how easily current flows.

Impedance matching

A transmitter delivers the most power when the load matches its impedance, usually 50 ohms. At radio frequencies you can match impedances with a transformer, a pi-network or a length of transmission line. All of these work.

Key facts

  • Reactance: opposition to AC from inductance or capacitance. Symbol X, measured in ohms.
  • Inductor: frequency up, reactance up. Capacitor: frequency up, reactance down.
  • Resonance: the reactances are equal and cancel. A series LC circuit has very low impedance at resonance.
  • Impedance = voltage ÷ current. Admittance = the inverse of impedance.
  • Matching at RF: transformer, pi-network or transmission line (all of these).

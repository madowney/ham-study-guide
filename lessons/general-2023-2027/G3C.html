HF signals go around the world by bouncing off the ionosphere. It's a layer of electrically charged gas high in the atmosphere, energized by the Sun. This lesson covers its layers, and why low bands die during the day.

The layers

  • D region: the closest to Earth. It exists only in daylight, and it absorbs signals rather than returning them.
  • E region: higher. It can return signals up to about 1,200 miles in one hop.
  • F1 and F2: highest. F2 gives the longest skip, because it is the highest: about 2,500 miles per hop. At night F1 and F2 merge into one F layer.

Why low bands die during the day

Sunlight creates the D region, and the D region absorbs signals below about 10 MHz. So during the day, long-distance contacts on 160, 80, 60 and 40 meters are hard. After sunset the D region fades away and these bands open up for long distances.

Critical frequency and angle

  • Critical frequency: the highest frequency that is refracted back to Earth at a given angle. Higher frequencies go straight through into space.
  • Critical angle: the highest takeoff angle that still returns a wave to Earth. Steeper than that, the wave escapes.

Skip zone and scatter

Between the end of ground-wave range and where the first sky-wave hop lands, there's a gap called the skip zone. Normally you can't be heard there.

Scatter propagation lets stations in the skip zone hear you anyway. Here's how scatter signals behave:

  • They're weak, because only a small part of the signal energy is scattered into the skip zone.
  • They sound distorted, because the energy arrives by several different paths.
  • They have a fluttering sound.

NVIS

Near vertical incidence skywave means firing the signal nearly straight up so it comes back down close by. It's short-distance MF or HF propagation at high elevation angles. That's great for covering a region a few hundred miles across, for example in emergency work, without a skip zone.

Key facts

  • D region: closest to Earth. It absorbs signals below 10 MHz in daylight, which makes 160–40 m hard during the day.
  • F2 is the highest region, so it gives the longest skip.
  • Critical frequency: highest frequency refracted back. Critical angle: highest takeoff angle that returns.
  • Scatter: heard in the skip zone. Weak (little energy), distorted (several paths), fluttering.
  • NVIS: short-range MF/HF at high elevation angles.

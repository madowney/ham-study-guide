HF is shared by everyone, with no frequency coordinator and no reservations. This lesson covers the etiquette that keeps it working, and the emergency rules that override everything else.

Nobody owns a frequency

Except during emergencies, no amateur station has priority access to any frequency. Not nets, not contests, not a conversation that started first. If propagation shifts and another group starts interfering with you, the good practice is to work it out with them in a mutually acceptable way.

Picking a frequency

  • Follow the voluntary band plan. It's a gentleman's agreement about which modes go where within a band.
  • Before calling CQ, ask whether the frequency is in use. On CW, send "QRL?" and your call sign. On phone, ask if the frequency is in use, then give your call sign.
  • Leave room for your neighbors:

CW150 to 500 HzA CW signal is very narrow
SSB2 to 3 kHzAbout the width of one SSB voice signal

One band-plan detail is on the exam. On 6 meters, 50.100 to 50.125 MHz is the DX window. Stations in the 48 contiguous states use it only for contacts with stations outside the 48 states.

Emergencies

  • If a station in distress breaks into your conversation, first acknowledge it and find out what help it needs. Don't go silent, and don't just turn down your power.
  • RACES, the Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service, supports government emergency management. Its control operator must hold an FCC-issued amateur license. A government official without a license can't be one.
  • RACES drills and tests can run up to 1 hour per week without special authorization.

Running a net

Good net management includes having a backup frequency in case of interference or poor conditions.

Key facts

  • No one has priority on a frequency, except in emergencies.
  • Interference after a propagation change: work it out with the other stations.
  • Follow the voluntary band plan. Ask "QRL?" (CW) or "Is the frequency in use?" (phone) with your call.
  • Spacing: CW 150–500 Hz, SSB 2–3 kHz.
  • 50.100–50.125 MHz: from the lower 48, only contacts outside the lower 48.
  • Station in distress: acknowledge it and find out what help it needs.
  • RACES control operator: FCC-licensed amateurs only. Drills up to 1 hour per week.
  • Nets: have a backup frequency.

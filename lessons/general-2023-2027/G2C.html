You don't need to know Morse code to pass, but you do need its vocabulary. CW operators use short codes, called Q signals and prosigns, so they don't have to spell everything out. This lesson is the phrasebook.

Q signals

A Q signal is three letters starting with Q. With a question mark, it asks a question. Without one, it answers or states something.

SignalMeaning
QRL?"Are you busy?" or "Is this frequency in use?"
QRSSend slower. If someone sends "QRS?", slow down.
QSLI have received and understood
QRNI am troubled by static (N for noise)
QRVI am ready to receive
QRPLow power (comes up in another lesson)

Prosigns

Prosigns are two letters sent run together as a single character.

ProsignMeaning
AREnd of a formal message
KNOver, but I'm listening only for a specific station (or stations). No one else should break in.
SKEnd of contact
BKBreak, back to you

CW technique

  • Answering a CQ: send at the fastest speed you're comfortable copying, but no faster than the CQ. The other station will match your speed.
  • Zero beat means matching your transmit frequency to the received signal, so you both end up on the same frequency.
  • Full break-in (QSK): the transmitter switches so quickly that you can hear between code characters and elements. Someone can interrupt you mid-word.
  • A "C" added to a signal report (like 579C) means your signal is chirpy or unstable. Its pitch slides as you key.

Key facts

  • QRL? = is the frequency in use? QRS = send slower. QSL = received and understood.
  • QRN = troubled by static. QRV = ready to receive.
  • AR = end of a formal message. KN = listening only for specific stations.
  • Answer a CQ no faster than it was sent.
  • Zero beat = match your transmit frequency to the received signal.
  • QSK = you can hear between characters and elements.
  • "C" in a report = chirpy signal.

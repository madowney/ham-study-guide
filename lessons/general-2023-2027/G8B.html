Almost every radio works by mixing signals together to shift them to a convenient frequency. This lesson covers mixers, the unwanted signals they create, and two FM calculations.

Mixers

Mixing two RF signals is called heterodyning. A mixer's output contains the sum and the difference of its two input frequencies.

A receiver mixes the incoming signal with its own local oscillator (LO) to land on a fixed intermediate frequency (IF). To tune different stations, the local oscillator is the input that is varied.

  • Image response: interference from a signal twice the IF away from the one you want. It mixes to the same IF from the other side.

Intermodulation

Intermodulation is when two signals combine in a non-linear circuit to produce unwanted spurious outputs.

  • Odd-order products are the troublemakers, because they land closest to the original frequencies.
  • 2F1 − F2 is an odd-order product (2 + 1 = 3, which is odd).

FM arithmetic

FM bandwidth: 2 × (deviation + modulating frequency).
5 kHz deviation and 3 kHz audio: 2 × (5 + 3) = 16 kHz.

Multipliers: VHF FM transmitters often generate a lower frequency and then use a multiplier stage, which makes a harmonic, to reach the operating frequency. Deviation gets multiplied along with frequency.

146.52 ÷ 12.21 = 12, so the oscillator needs only 5 kHz ÷ 12 = 416.7 Hz of deviation.

Practical points

  • Duty cycle matters because some modes, like FT8 and RTTY, transmit at full power the whole time. That could exceed your transmitter's average power rating.
  • Match the receiver bandwidth to the mode for the best signal-to-noise ratio.
  • Higher symbol rates need wider bandwidth.

Key facts

  • Heterodyning = mixing. The output is the sum and difference. Tune by varying the local oscillator.
  • Image response: a signal twice the IF away.
  • Intermodulation: a non-linear circuit makes spurious products. Odd-order products are closest. 2F1 − F2 is odd-order.
  • FM bandwidth: 5 kHz deviation + 3 kHz audio = 16 kHz.
  • Multiplier: makes a harmonic. 12.21 MHz × 12 = 146.52 MHz, so deviation is 416.7 Hz.
  • High duty cycle can exceed the average power rating. Matched receiver bandwidth gives the best SNR. Higher symbol rate means wider bandwidth.

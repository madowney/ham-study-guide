This lesson covers transformers and what happens when you combine resistors, capacitors and inductors. There's one trick to remember: capacitors combine "backwards" from the other two.

Transformers

A transformer is two coils of wire sharing a core. AC in the first coil (the primary) induces voltage in the second (the secondary) through mutual inductance.

  • The voltage scales with the turns ratio. 500 turns in, 1500 turns out, is 3 times the turns, so 120 V AC × 3 = 360 V.
  • Run a transformer backwards and the ratio flips. Feed a 4:1 step-down transformer from its secondary, and the input voltage is multiplied by 4.
  • Power in roughly equals power out, so the low-voltage side carries more current. In a step-up transformer, the primary uses heavier wire to handle its higher current.
  • Impedance scales with the square of the turns ratio. To match 600 Ω to 50 Ω: 600 ÷ 50 = 12, and √12 ≈ 3.5 to 1.

Capacitors do the opposite of resistors and inductors. Two equal parts combined the "reciprocal" way give half of one; three equal parts give a third.

The reciprocal formula: 1 ÷ (1/A + 1/B + 1/C). For just two parts, a shortcut: (A × B) ÷ (A + B).

Key facts

  • Transformer action: mutual inductance. Voltage follows the turns ratio: 500:1500 turns with 120 V in gives 360 V.
  • A 4:1 step-down transformer fed backwards multiplies the voltage by 4.
  • Step-up primary wire is thicker because it carries more current.
  • Impedance ratio = turns ratio squared. 600 Ω to 50 Ω: 3.5 to 1.
  • Resistors and inductors add in series. Capacitors add in parallel.
  • 5.9 Ω · 67 Ω · 10.750 nF · 33.3 µF · 14.3 µF · 3.3 mH · 70 mH.
  • More capacitance: capacitor in parallel. More inductance: inductor in series.

This lesson collects operating know-how for HF: calling CQ, pointing a beam the long way around the world, keeping a log, and how hams police themselves.

Making contacts

  • Calling CQ (looking for anyone): say "CQ" a few times, then "this is," then your call sign a few times. Pause to listen, and repeat.
  • Signal reports are exchanged at the start so each station can adjust to conditions. If you're weak, the other station knows to keep it short or slow down.
  • QRP means low-power operation, typically 5 watts or less.
  • Use the NATO phonetic alphabet: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta. "Able, Baker" and "Adam, Boy" are old systems.
  • In a contest, the only legal requirement is to identify your station according to normal FCC rules. Submitting logs and sending QSL cards are optional.

Short path and long path

A radio signal can reach a distant station two ways around the globe. The short path is the usual direct route. The long path goes the other way around, and sometimes it's the open one. To use it, point your beam antenna 180 degrees from the short-path heading.

To find headings, hams use an azimuthal projection map. It's centered on your location and shows true bearings and distances from there. Land shapes look distorted, but every straight line from the center is a true great-circle path.

Keeping a log

The FCC no longer requires most logs. Many amateurs keep one anyway, to help answer if the FCC asks about their station's operation.

The Volunteer Monitor Program

This program is made up of amateur volunteers formally enlisted to monitor the airwaves for rule violations. The goal is to encourage amateurs to self-regulate and follow the rules.

To find a station whose stuck carrier is holding a repeater on, monitors compare beam headings on the repeater's input from several locations. Where the lines cross is where the station is.

Key facts

  • CQ: "CQ" a few times, "this is," your call sign a few times, then listen.
  • Signal reports let each station adjust to conditions. QRP = low power.
  • NATO phonetics: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta.
  • Contests: only normal FCC identification is required.
  • Long path: point your antenna 180° from the short path.
  • Azimuthal map: true bearings and distances from one location.
  • Logs: helpful if the FCC asks about your station.
  • Volunteer Monitors: enlisted volunteers who watch for rule violations to encourage self-regulation. They locate a stuck carrier by comparing beam headings on the repeater input.

You'll be able to run up to 1500 watts, so RF safety stops being theoretical. This lesson covers how RF affects the body and the steps the FCC requires to keep people safe.

What RF does

RF energy heats body tissue, like a microwave oven does. It's non-ionizing: no radiation poisoning, no damage to DNA.

Exposure is determined from all of these: the signal's duty cycle, its frequency and its power density.

Time averaging and duty cycle

  • Limits use time averaging: the total RF exposure averaged over a certain period. A brief peak matters less than steady exposure.
  • So a lower duty cycle permits higher transmit power. CW and SSB voice are off much of the time; FT8 and FM are on continuously.
  • The rules cover all stations with a time-averaged transmission of more than one milliwatt, amateur stations included.

Checking your station

  1. See if your station meets the FCC's exemption criteria, which depend on power, frequency and distance.
  2. If it doesn't, perform an RF exposure evaluation following FCC OET Bulletin 65.
  3. You can show compliance in any of these ways: calculation from OET Bulletin 65, computer modeling, or measuring field strength.
  4. For measurements, use a calibrated field strength meter with a calibrated antenna.

Required in general: do a routine RF exposure evaluation and keep people out of any high-exposure areas you find.

If you're over the limit

  • You must take action to prevent people from being exposed to the excessive fields.
  • If your beam's main lobe could expose a neighbor above the limit, make sure the antenna can't be pointed toward them when they're there.
  • With an indoor antenna, make sure MPE limits aren't exceeded in occupied areas.

Key facts

  • RF heats tissue. Exposure depends on duty cycle, frequency and power density (all of these).
  • Time averaging: total exposure averaged over a period. Lower duty cycle permits more power.
  • Rules cover every station averaging over 1 milliwatt.
  • Not exempt: evaluate per OET Bulletin 65. Calculation, modeling or measurement all work.
  • Measure with a calibrated field strength meter and calibrated antenna.
  • Over the limit: prevent exposure. Neighbor in the main lobe: don't point the beam at them. Indoor antenna: keep occupied areas under MPE.

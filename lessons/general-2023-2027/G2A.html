Voice on HF almost always means single sideband (SSB). This lesson explains what SSB is, and the one rule that settles most of these questions: which sideband to use.

What single sideband is

An AM signal has three parts: a carrier in the middle and two copies of your voice, one on each side. These copies are called sidebands: the upper sideband (above the carrier) and the lower sideband (below it).

Both sidebands carry the same voice, and the carrier carries none. So SSB sends only one sideband, with the other sideband and the carrier suppressed. That gives it two advantages over other analog voice modes: less bandwidth and greater power efficiency. All your power goes into the part that carries your voice. That's why single sideband is the most common voice mode on HF.

Upper or lower?

WhereSideband
160, 75/80 and 40 meters (below 10 MHz)Lower (LSB)
14 MHz and higher: 20, 17, 15, 12, 10 metersUpper (USB)
SSB on VHF and UHFUpper (USB)

Rule: below 10 MHz, lower sideband. Everything else, upper. And why LSB down low? Not physics, not law. It's simply commonly accepted amateur practice.

Operating a phone station

  • To break into an ongoing contact, say your call sign once.
  • VOX (voice-operated transmit) keys the radio when you talk. Its advantage is hands-free operation. The alternative is PTT, push-to-talk.
  • ALC (automatic level control) keeps the transmitter from being overdriven. To set it properly, adjust the transmit audio or microphone gain.
  • When a station in the lower 48 states calls "CQ DX", it wants distant contacts. Only stations outside the lower 48 states should answer.

Key facts

  • SSB: one sideband sent; the carrier and other sideband are suppressed. Less bandwidth, more power efficiency.
  • SSB is the most common HF voice mode.
  • LSB on 160, 75 and 40 m, because it's accepted practice.
  • USB at 14 MHz and up (including 17 and 12 m), and for VHF/UHF SSB.
  • Breaking in: say your call sign once.
  • VOX: hands-free. ALC: adjust mic gain.
  • "CQ DX" from the US: answer only if you're outside the lower 48.

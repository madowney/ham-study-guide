This lesson covers HF from a car and power from the sun. Both are about doing a lot with limited resources.

HF mobile

The biggest limit on HF mobile is the efficiency of the electrically short antenna. A full-size 40-meter antenna is 33 feet long, and a car whip is a few feet.

  • A shortened antenna's drawback: very limited operating bandwidth. You re-tune often.
  • A capacitance hat, a set of spokes near the top, electrically lengthens a physically short antenna.
  • A corona ball on the tip reduces RF voltage discharge from the tip. The tip of a short antenna can reach very high voltage.

Powering a 100-watt radio in a car:

  • The best connection is straight to the battery with heavy-gauge wire, fused.
  • Don't use the cigarette-lighter socket: its wiring may not handle the current. A 100-watt radio draws about 20 amps.
  • Receive noise can come from all of these: the charging system, the fuel system and the car's computers.

Solar power

  • A fully lit silicon solar cell gives about 0.5 volts with no load. That's why panels wire many cells in series-parallel: series for voltage, parallel for current.
  • Put a series diode between the panel and the battery so the battery can't discharge back through the panel in the dark.
  • With a lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) battery, the panel must have a charge controller.

Key facts

  • HF mobile's biggest limit: the short antenna's efficiency. Its drawback: narrow bandwidth.
  • Capacitance hat: electrically lengthens. Corona ball: reduces discharge from the tip.
  • Power: direct to the battery with heavy, fused wire. Not the accessory socket, whose wiring may be too light.
  • Vehicle noise sources: all of these.
  • Solar cell about 0.5 V; cells wired series-parallel. Series diode stops discharge at night. LiFePO4 needs a charge controller.

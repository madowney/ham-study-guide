This lesson covers the rules for passing messages for other people, for stations that run themselves, and for a few frequencies you should stay off.

Third-party traffic

A "third party" is someone who isn't the control operator but sends a message through your station. For example, a friend says hello on the air while you operate.

  • A third party is disqualified if their own amateur license was revoked and not reinstated. Being a non-citizen or speaking another language doesn't disqualify anyone.
  • With a country that has a third-party agreement with the US, messages must be about amateur radio, personal remarks, or about emergencies or disaster relief. No business.
  • Third-party messages can go through a remote-controlled station whenever the FCC rules allow third-party messages at all.

Automatic control and repeaters

  • A 10-meter repeater may retransmit a 2-meter signal from a Technician only if the 10-meter repeater's control operator holds at least a General license. The license that matters is the one at the transmitter putting the signal on HF.
  • Automatically controlled digital stations may talk to each other anywhere on 6 meters and shorter wavelengths, and in limited segments of some HF bands.
  • To contact an automatic digital station outside those segments, the station starting the contact must be under local or remote control, with a person at the controls.

Avoiding interference

You must take extra steps to avoid harmful interference in all three cases on the exam, so the answer is all these choices are correct:

  • operating within one mile of an FCC monitoring station
  • using a band where amateurs are secondary
  • transmitting spread spectrum

Spread spectrum is limited to 10 watts PEP.

Avoid transmitting on 14.100, 18.110, 21.150, 24.930 and 28.200 MHz. A worldwide system of propagation beacons runs on those frequencies.

Odds and ends

  • North and South America are in ITU Region 2, so Region 2 frequency allocations apply.
  • Amateur stations may talk to unlicensed Wi-Fi devices in no part of the 2.4 GHz band, even though the frequencies overlap.

Key facts

  • A third party is disqualified only if their amateur license was revoked.
  • Third-party agreement countries: messages about amateur radio, personal remarks or emergencies only.
  • 10 m repeater relaying a Technician: needs a General-or-higher control operator.
  • Automatic digital stations: 6 m and up, plus limited HF segments. Outside them, the station starting the contact must be under local or remote control.
  • Spread spectrum: 10 W PEP max. Take extra care near FCC monitoring stations, on secondary bands, and with spread spectrum.
  • Beacon frequencies: 14.100, 18.110, 21.150, 24.930 and 28.200 MHz.
  • The Americas = ITU Region 2. Amateurs may not contact Wi-Fi stations on any part of 2.4 GHz.

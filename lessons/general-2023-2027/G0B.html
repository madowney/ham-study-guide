This lesson covers safety beyond RF: house wiring, lightning, towers, generators and solder. Most of it is common sense, plus a few numbers.

Wiring and breakers

  • Fuses and breakers go on only the hot wires. Never on the neutral or ground.
  • Match wire size to breaker. Smaller AWG numbers mean thicker wire:
WireBreaker
AWG 1415 A
AWG 1220 A
  • A GFCI cuts the power when current flows from a hot wire directly to ground, possibly through a person.
  • The National Electrical Code covers the electrical safety of your station. It doesn't cover bandwidth, modulation or RF exposure.
  • A power supply interlock removes dangerous voltages when the cabinet is opened.

Lightning

  • The lightning ground system goes outside the building.
  • All ground rods must be bonded together with all other grounds.
  • Lightning arrestors go where the feed lines enter the building.

Towers

  • Check that your safety harness is rated for the climber's weight and within its service life.
  • If the tower carries powered equipment, make sure all circuits feeding it are locked out and tagged before you climb.

Generators and solder

  • Run a generator in a well-ventilated area, because its exhaust contains carbon monoxide.
  • Lead-tin solder: lead can get into food if you don't wash your hands carefully after handling it.

Key facts

  • Fuse only the hot wires. AWG 14 → 15 A. AWG 12 → 20 A.
  • GFCI trips on current from hot to ground. NEC covers station electrical safety. Interlock cuts voltage when the case is opened.
  • Lightning ground: outside, with all grounds bonded together. Arrestors where feed lines enter.
  • Harness: rated for your weight and within its service life. Lock out and tag the tower's power.
  • Generator: ventilation. Solder: wash your hands.

This lesson covers three practical skills: making your voice punch through with a speech processor, reading an S meter, and staying inside the band edges on SSB.

Speech processors

  • A speech processor increases the apparent loudness of your voice.
  • It works by raising average power; peak power stays the same.
  • Set it wrong and you get all of these: distorted speech, extra intermodulation products and too much background noise.

S meters

An S meter measures received signal strength, on a scale from S1 to S9 and then "dB over S9."

  • One S unit = 6 dB, which is 4 times the power.
  • To go from S8 to S9 at a distant station, raise your power about 4 times.
  • 20 dB over S9 is 100 times more powerful than S9. (10 dB = 10×, 20 dB = 100×.)

Doubling your power gains only 3 dB, half an S unit. That's why a big amplifier makes less difference than you'd expect.

Staying inside the band

Your radio displays the suppressed carrier frequency, but your voice occupies about 3 kHz to one side of it:

  • LSB extends below the displayed frequency. So stay at least 3 kHz above the lower edge of a phone segment.
  • USB extends above it. So stay at least 3 kHz below the upper edge.
  • That's why 14.347 MHz on USB is the highest you'd set on 20 meters: the signal reaches exactly 14.350, the top of the band.

Key facts

  • Speech processor: louder, by raising average power. Misadjusted: all of these problems.
  • S meter: received signal strength. 1 S unit = 6 dB = 4× power. S8 to S9: 4× power. S9+20 dB = 100×.
  • LSB at 7.178: 7.175–7.178 MHz. USB at 14.347: 14.347–14.350 MHz.
  • LSB: at least 3 kHz above the lower edge. USB: at least 3 kHz below the upper edge.

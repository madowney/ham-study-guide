A power supply turns wall AC into the steady DC a radio needs. This lesson walks through how, and then covers the one schematic figure on the General exam.

Rectifiers: AC to pulsing DC

Diodes let current flow only one way, so they chop AC into one-direction pulses.

  • Half-wave: needs only one diode. It uses 180° of each cycle.
  • Full-wave: uses all 360°. The center-tapped version uses two diodes and a center-tapped transformer. (A bridge uses four diodes.)
  • Unfiltered full-wave output is a series of DC pulses at twice the frequency of the AC input.

Smoothing and safety

  • The filter that smooths those pulses into steady DC uses capacitors and inductors.
  • A bleeder resistor discharges the filter capacitors when the power is off. Big capacitors can hold a deadly charge long after you unplug the supply.
  • A switchmode supply switches at a high frequency, which allows smaller components. It's lighter and smaller than a linear supply.

Key facts

  • Half-wave: one diode, 180°. Full-wave: 360°. Center-tapped full-wave: two diodes.
  • Unfiltered full-wave: DC pulses at twice the input frequency.
  • Filter: capacitors and inductors. Bleeder resistor: discharges the capacitors at power-off.
  • Switchmode: high frequency, smaller components.

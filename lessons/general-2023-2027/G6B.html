This lesson covers more parts: ferrite cores, integrated circuits, and the connectors you'll use to hook everything together.

Ferrite

Ferrite is a ceramic magnetic material. It's used for cores in coils and as beads that snap onto cables.

  • How a ferrite performs at different frequencies depends on its composition, or "mix".
  • A toroid is a doughnut-shaped ferrite core. Its advantages are all of these: large inductance values are possible, the core can be chosen for a frequency range, and most of the magnetic field stays inside the core.
  • A ferrite bead on a coax cable reduces common-mode current on the shield by creating an impedance in that current's path. It chokes off unwanted current that would otherwise flow on the outside of the shield.

Integrated circuits

  • MMIC = Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit. It's a tiny amplifier block for high frequencies.
  • CMOS logic's advantage over TTL is low power consumption.
  • An operational amplifier (op-amp) is an analog device.
  • An LED gives off light when it's forward biased.

Key facts

  • Ferrite performance depends on its mix. Toroid advantages: all of these.
  • A ferrite bead stops common-mode current by adding impedance.
  • MMIC: Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit. CMOS beats TTL on power use. Op-amp: analog.
  • LED lights when forward biased.
  • BNC: to 4 GHz. Type N: moisture-resistant, to 10 GHz. SMA: small, threaded, several GHz. RCA: low frequency and DC.

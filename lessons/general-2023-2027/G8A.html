Modulation is how information rides on a radio wave. You can change the wave's strength, its frequency or its phase. This lesson names each method and covers what goes wrong when you overdo it.

The three things you can change

Change the…Name
Instantaneous power level (amplitude)Amplitude modulation (AM)
Instantaneous frequencyFrequency modulation (FM)
Phase anglePhase modulation (PM)
  • A reactance modulator on a transmitter's RF amplifier stage produces phase modulation.
  • Of the voice modes, single sideband uses the narrowest bandwidth.

AM and overdoing it

  • The modulation envelope of an AM signal is the shape you get by connecting the peaks of the modulated signal. It traces your voice.
  • Overmodulation causes excessive bandwidth: your signal splatters onto nearby frequencies.
  • Flat-topping is distortion caused by excessive drive or speech levels. The peaks get clipped flat. Turn down the mic gain.

Digital modulation

  • Direct binary FSK is made by changing an oscillator's frequency directly with a digital control signal.
  • FT8 uses 8-tone frequency-shift keying.
  • QPSK uses four phase shifts, 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees, each representing a pair of bits.
  • QPSK31: all of these are true. It's sideband-sensitive, its encoding provides error correction, and its bandwidth is about the same as BPSK31.

Link budget

Engineers plan a radio link like a household budget, in decibels.

  • Link budget: transmit power plus antenna gains, minus system losses, as seen at the receiver.
  • Link margin: the received power level minus the minimum signal the receiver needs. It's your safety cushion against fading.

Key facts

  • AM varies power. FM varies frequency. PM varies phase. A reactance modulator makes PM.
  • SSB is the narrowest voice mode.
  • Modulation envelope: connect the peaks. Overmodulation: excessive bandwidth. Flat-topping: too much drive or speech level.
  • Direct FSK: the digital signal shifts the oscillator. FT8: 8-tone FSK.
  • QPSK: 0/90/180/270° for pairs of bits. QPSK31: all of these.
  • Link budget: TX power + antenna gains − losses at the receiver. Link margin: received level − minimum required.

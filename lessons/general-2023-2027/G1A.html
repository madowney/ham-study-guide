The General license is the big upgrade: it opens most of every HF band, which is where you can talk across oceans. This lesson is the map of where you can transmit, and the few places you can't.

Your HF privileges

As a General you get all of five HF bands, and part of four others. The parts you don't get are reserved for Amateur Extra (and old Advanced) licensees.

The patterns the exam tests

  • Bands with Extra-only segments, and so bands where Generals can't use every frequency: 80, 40, 20 and 15 meters. Memorize that four-band list; it answers two questions.
  • When you only get part of a phone segment, you get the upper part. Extras get the bottom of each voice segment.
  • So 7.125 to 7.175 MHz is off limits to Generals: it's the bottom of the 40-meter phone segment.
  • 21,300 kHz (21.300 MHz) is inside the General 15-meter phone segment. The other choices in that question are on 20, 17 and 12 meters.
  • 30 meters is the band where both phone and image transmissions are prohibited. It's a narrow 50 kHz band shared with other services, so it's CW and data only.
  • 10 meters: General CW is allowed on the entire band. Repeaters may only use the portion above 29.5 MHz.

Memory hook: the "big four" contest bands, 80, 40, 20 and 15, are the ones carved up by license class. The WARC bands (30, 17, 12) and 160 and 10 are open to Generals end to end.

Secondary users

On some bands, amateurs are a secondary service. That means two things: amateur stations must not cause harmful interference to primary users, and they must accept interference from primary users.

Key facts

  • Bands with Extra-only segments (Generals can't use all of them): 80, 40, 20 and 15 meters.
  • Generals get the upper part of a voice segment.
  • 7.125–7.175 MHz: not for Generals. 21.300 MHz: OK.
  • 30 meters: no phone, no image transmissions.
  • 10 meters: CW anywhere on the band. Repeaters above 29.5 MHz.
  • Secondary user: don't interfere with primary users, and accept their interference.

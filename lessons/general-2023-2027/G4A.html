An HF transceiver's front panel is covered in knobs. This lesson explains what each one is for, and how to tune an old-school tube amplifier.

Receiving controls

ControlWhat it does
Notch filterRemoves a carrier (a steady tone) in the receiver passband
Noise blankerCuts receiver gain during each noise pulse, like ignition clicks
Noise reduction (DSP)Turn it up too far and signals get distorted
AttenuatorPrevents receiver overload from very strong signals
Reverse sideband on CWInterference may move off to the other side, so you may reduce or eliminate it
Dual VFOsTransmit on one frequency and listen on another ("split" operation, common when chasing rare DX)

Transmitting controls

  • An electronic keyer generates the dots and dashes automatically for CW. You hold the paddle; it times them.
  • ALC with a power amplifier prevents excessive drive.
  • For AFSK data modes like FT8, keep ALC inactive by turning the audio down, because ALC action distorts the signal.
  • With an external amplifier, the radio delays its RF output after keying the amplifier. That gives the amplifier time to switch its antenna relay.
  • An antenna tuner increases power transfer from the transmitter to the feed line. It doesn't change the SWR on the feed line itself.

Tuning a tube amplifier

  1. Adjust TUNE for a pronounced dip in plate current. That's resonance.
  2. Adjust LOAD (or COUPLING) for the desired power output without exceeding the maximum allowed plate current.

Key facts

  • Notch: removes a carrier. Noise blanker: cuts gain during pulses. Too much noise reduction: distortion.
  • Attenuator: prevents overload. Reverse sideband on CW: may dodge interference.
  • Dual VFO: transmit on one frequency, listen on another.
  • Keyer: makes dots and dashes. ALC: prevents overdrive; keep it inactive on AFSK.
  • RF delay: lets the amplifier switch its antenna.
  • Tuner: more power transfer into the feed line.
  • Tube amp: TUNE for a plate-current dip; LOAD for desired power within the plate-current limit.

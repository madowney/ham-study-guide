The Sun runs HF radio. Its activity sets how strongly the ionosphere is energized, so it decides which bands are open. This lesson covers the solar cycle, solar storms, and the numbers hams watch.

Sunspots and the solar cycle

Sunspot numbers rise and fall over a roughly 11-year cycle.

  • More sunspots mean a stronger ionosphere, so there's a better chance of good propagation on the higher frequencies.
  • In low solar activity, the least reliable bands for long distance are 15, 12 and 10 meters.
  • 20 meters supports worldwide daytime propagation at any point in the cycle. It's the dependable workhorse band.
  • Conditions also vary on a 26- to 28-day cycle, because the Sun rotates. Active regions come back into view about once a month.

K and A are about Earth's magnetic field, not the Sun's. Remember them as K for quicK (short-term) and A for Average (long-term).

When the Sun erupts

Different solar events reach Earth at different speeds. The exam asks about all three:

EventWhat arrivesTravel timeEffect
Solar flareUV and X-rays, at the speed of light8 minutesSudden ionospheric disturbance
Coronal mass ejection (CME)A cloud of charged particles15 hours to several daysGeomagnetic storm
Coronal holeA stream of charged particlesDaysHF is disturbed
  • A sudden ionospheric disturbance hits the daylight side and disrupts lower frequencies more than higher ones.
  • A geomagnetic storm is a temporary disturbance in Earth's geomagnetic field. It degrades high-latitude HF propagation, meaning paths over the poles.
  • There's an upside: high geomagnetic activity creates auroras that can reflect VHF signals.

Key facts

  • More sunspots: better propagation on higher frequencies.
  • Low solar activity: 15, 12 and 10 m are least reliable. 20 m works worldwide in daylight at any point in the cycle.
  • 26–28 day cycle: the Sun's rotation.
  • Solar flux: 10.7 cm radio emission. K-index: Earth's field, short term. A-index: Earth's field, long term.
  • Flare: 8 minutes, hits lower frequencies hardest. CME: 15 hours to several days. Coronal holes: HF disturbed.
  • Geomagnetic storm: degrades high-latitude HF. Auroras can reflect VHF.
